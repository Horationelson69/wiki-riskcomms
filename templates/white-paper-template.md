# {{title}}

## Executive Summary

What problem this paper addresses, what it proposes, and why existing approaches fall short. Keep this to one tight paragraph — it should work as a standalone abstract.

## 1. Context and Problem

The operational or field reality that motivates the paper. Ground this in specific, citable conditions rather than general assertion.

## 2. Why the Issue Matters

Evidence that the problem is real and current — citing studies, standards bodies, or documented incidents. Flag any statistic without a verifiable source as a directional estimate rather than presenting it as settled fact.

## 3. Risks and Challenges

Enumerate the specific failure modes the paper's proposal is designed to address. One descriptive paragraph per risk, naming the mechanism, not just the symptom.

## 4. Strategic Analysis

### 4.1 Conceptual Foundations

What existing frameworks, theories, or established practices the paper's proposal draws on. Cite the source framework by name and explain precisely what it contributes.

### 4.2 The Governing Principle

The single sentence or short statement that anchors the entire proposal — the idea everything else operationalises.

## 5. The Framework

The core proposal itself, structured and named. Use a table if the framework has discrete stages, tiers, or components.

## 6. Operational Implications

What changes in practice if an organisation adopts this — concrete, practitioner-facing consequences, not abstract principle.

## 7. Practical Recommendations

Numbered, actionable. Each one specific enough that a reader could act on it without further interpretation.

## 9. Final Observations

Close the argument — what's at stake if the proposal isn't adopted, and what's invited from practitioners who use it.
